# Literature, venue and metric reconnaissance

Load before full-paper drafting. For a narrow language-only revision, do not add an unrelated literature project. This module supplies a research-writing procedure, not a preselected bibliography for an unknown study.

For a scientific audit or substantive research decision, load [research reasoning](research-reasoning.en.md). Reuse the records below. Language-only edits do not require the full review.

## R44 Search from the actual study and read the sources

First identify what the researcher actually built, measured, derived or observed. Distinguish the research question, population, intervention or system, setting and available records. Confirm versions and missing originals with the researcher. Do not search for a plausible experiment to substitute for their work.

Use available web or scholarly retrieval for related work, methodological foundations, current official venue instructions and representative papers. State tool access first. Without network access, label the search incomplete and use only supplied sources with their access limits. Never claim a new search was performed from model memory. Ask for relevant PDFs or permission to use an available retrieval route when blocked.

Keep a search log with query, date, database or engine, inclusion reason, exclusions, language and time limits. Use primary sources, official templates, original metric or instrument descriptions, and full papers where accessible. Include recent close comparators and necessary foundational work. Do not impose an arbitrary citation quota, copy a competitor's bibliography, or use star counts and prestige as evidence of fit. A targeted writing search is not an exhaustive systematic review.

Open selected sources rather than relying on a search snippet or AI summary. Record title, authors, date/version, canonical URL or DOI, access level, and exact page, section, table or equation locator. Distinguish full-text, abstract-only, metadata-only and unavailable sources. Bibliographic identity verification does not establish claim support. Check corrections, retraction notices and preprint versus published differences when relevant. Never bypass paywalls or access controls.

Use screenshots/page images when inspecting PDF figures, tables, equations or layout. Parsed prose alone cannot establish how the paper looks. Mark inaccessible details as not inspected. If private data would leave the approved environment, obtain permission first and minimize the query. Do not upload an unpublished manuscript or participant records just to improve a search.

## R45 Learn conventions without copying another paper

Extract a literature matrix containing the question, design, inputs, populations, metrics, comparisons, contribution type, section organization, visual roles, limitations and source locators. Separate observed conventions from recommendations. Treat a missing report as not reported, not proof that a capability is absent.

Check the official venue's current template, page limits, citation style, anonymization, AI disclosure and figure policy. A published exemplar may be a camera-ready version, another track or a different year. It is not the authority for the current submission format. Resolve conflicts against the verified target instructions and the researcher. Do not automatically select APA, IEEE or a particular font just because a tool defaults to it.

Use exemplars to learn reporting detail and argument functions, not to imitate distinctive sentences or reproduce a figure layout panel-for-panel. Cite the scientific ideas actually used. Keep the user's no-contraction, punctuation, Introduction and Contributions rules unless an explicit conflict is resolved. Familiar academic wording is not automatically AI prose. Do not copy another paper's unsupported claims, metric values or novelty framing.

For system overviews, learn which relationships readers need, then map this study's real nodes and edges. For plots, learn appropriate encodings and reporting conventions, then compute from this study's approved data. Other papers' images are not originals from this experiment. Obtain originals from the researcher or propose a clearly labeled conceptual alternative only after asking.

## R46 Decide metrics before presenting results

For each candidate metric, link its research question and primary definition source. Record the formula, independent unit, denominator, domain, direction, units, aggregation, repeated-measure structure, missing/excluded data policy, uncertainty calculation, assumptions and compatible comparator protocol. Same metric name does not imply the same definition. Do not infer an unreported protocol from the shape of a figure.

Map required inputs to actual raw fields, code revision, functions and approved result exports. Classify each metric as selected, adapted, rejected, proposed, not measured or not applicable in the working plan. Explain selection and rejection, not just popularity. Obtain the researcher's approval before changing the study's method, endpoints or analysis. Code access is not evidence that code ran.

A literature metric is a candidate, not a study result. No raw inputs means no fabricated value. A new calculation from existing data must be actually run, checked against raw records, and labeled derived. Separate prespecified analyses from post hoc exploration. Never claim retrospective preregistration, cherry-pick favorable metrics, tune on test results, or compare published results across incompatible populations, splits, units and protocols as though they were one benchmark.

For qualitative or theoretical work, document the applicable evidence or validity criteria instead of inventing numeric metrics. For validated instruments, inspect original scoring, adaptation and permission requirements before recommending their use. When an instrument was never collected, state not measured and propose future collection rather than fabricate questionnaire results.

Use [search-log.csv](../assets/search-log.csv), [literature-matrix.csv](../assets/literature-matrix.csv) and [metric-plan.csv](../assets/metric-plan.csv) only when their records are needed. Reuse ARS registers by cross-reference when they already contain these fields. Freeze the approved definitions before final tables, plots and abstract. Recompute all linked artifacts after an authorized definition change.
